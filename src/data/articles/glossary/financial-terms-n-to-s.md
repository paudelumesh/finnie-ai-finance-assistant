In the world of personal finance, understanding key terms can significantly enhance your financial literacy. This glossary covers essential financial terms from N to S, providing clear definitions and examples to help you grasp their meanings and applications.

## Net Worth

Net worth is a measure of your financial health, calculated by subtracting your total liabilities (what you owe) from your total assets (what you own). 

For example, if you own a home worth $300,000, have $50,000 in savings, and owe $200,000 on your mortgage and $10,000 in credit card debt, your net worth would be:

- Total Assets: $300,000 (home) + $50,000 (savings) = $350,000
- Total Liabilities: $200,000 (mortgage) + $10,000 (credit card debt) = $210,000
- Net Worth: $350,000 - $210,000 = $140,000

A positive net worth indicates that your assets exceed your liabilities, which is generally a sign of good financial health.

## Retirement Accounts

Retirement accounts are savings plans designed to help you save for retirement while offering tax advantages. Common types include 401(k) plans and Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs). 

For instance, if you contribute $5,000 to a traditional IRA, you may be able to deduct that amount from your taxable income, potentially lowering your tax bill for the year. Additionally, the money in these accounts can grow tax-deferred until you withdraw it in retirement.

## Savings Rate

The savings rate is the percentage of your income that you save rather than spend. It is an important indicator of your financial habits and can help you reach your financial goals.

For example, if you earn $50,000 a year and save $5,000, your savings rate would be:

- Savings Rate = (Savings / Income) x 100
- Savings Rate = ($5,000 / $50,000) x 100 = 10%

A higher savings rate can lead to greater financial security and help you build wealth over time.

## Stock Market

The stock market is a collection of markets where shares of publicly traded companies are bought and sold. It serves as a platform for companies to raise capital and for investors to potentially earn returns on their investments.

For example, if you buy 10 shares of a company at $20 each, your total investment would be $200. If the stock price rises to $30, your shares would be worth $300, giving you a profit of $100 if you sold them.

## Taxes

Taxes are mandatory financial charges imposed by the government on individuals and businesses. They fund public services and infrastructure. Understanding how taxes work is crucial for effective financial planning.

For instance, if you earn $50,000 in a year and are subject to a 20% income tax rate, you would owe $10,000 in taxes. This means your take-home pay would be $40,000 after taxes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Net Worth**: The difference between your assets and liabilities, indicating financial health.
- **Retirement Accounts**: Tax-advantaged savings plans for retirement, such as 401(k)s and IRAs.
- **Savings Rate**: The percentage of income saved, reflecting financial habits.
- **Stock Market**: A marketplace for buying and selling shares of companies, offering investment opportunities.
- **Taxes**: Mandatory payments to the government that fund public services, impacting your net income.

Understanding these financial terms can empower you to make informed decisions about your money and future.
