Financial terms can often feel overwhelming, especially for beginners. This glossary aims to clarify some key financial terms that start with the letters G through M. Understanding these terms can help you make informed decisions about your finances.

## Gross Income

Gross income is the total amount of money you earn before any deductions, such as taxes, retirement contributions, or health insurance premiums. For example, if you earn $50,000 a year, that amount is your gross income. It’s important to know your gross income because it is used to calculate your tax liability and determine eligibility for loans or financial aid.

## Interest

Interest is the cost of borrowing money or the earnings from lending it. When you take out a loan, the lender charges you interest as a fee for using their money. Conversely, when you deposit money in a savings account, the bank pays you interest for keeping your money with them. 

For example, if you borrow $1,000 at an annual interest rate of 5%, you will owe $1,050 at the end of the year if you don’t make any payments during that time. The $50 is the interest charged for borrowing the money.

## Investment

An investment is an asset or item acquired with the goal of generating income or appreciation. Common types of investments include stocks, bonds, real estate, and mutual funds. 

For instance, if you invest $1,000 in a stock that appreciates by 10% over a year, your investment will be worth $1,100 at the end of that year. Investments can be risky, and it’s important to research and understand what you are investing in.

## Liability

A liability is a financial obligation or debt that you owe to another party. This can include loans, mortgages, credit card debt, and any other financial commitments. Understanding your liabilities is crucial for managing your overall financial health.

For example, if you have a mortgage of $200,000 and a car loan of $20,000, your total liabilities amount to $220,000. Keeping track of your liabilities helps you understand how much you owe and can influence your budgeting and saving strategies.

## Mutual Fund

A mutual fund is an investment vehicle that pools money from multiple investors to purchase a diversified portfolio of stocks, bonds, or other securities. This allows individual investors to access a broader range of investments than they might be able to afford on their own.

For example, if you invest $1,000 in a mutual fund that holds 50 different stocks, your investment is spread across those stocks, reducing the risk associated with investing in a single company. Mutual funds are managed by professional fund managers who make decisions about which securities to buy and sell.

## Key Takeaways

- **Gross Income**: Total earnings before deductions.
- **Interest**: Cost of borrowing or earnings from lending money.
- **Investment**: Asset acquired to generate income or appreciation.
- **Liability**: Financial obligations or debts owed to others.
- **Mutual Fund**: Investment vehicle pooling money from multiple investors for diversified holdings.

Understanding these financial terms can empower you to make better financial decisions and improve your overall financial literacy.
